The Microsoft outage, November 2026

From an earlier discussion about our digital dependencies to a global outage and the conversations that followed.

In October 2026, I wrote an article for The Conversation exploring what might happen if the internet went down. My argument was that while a complete internet shutdown is highly unlikely, many of the digital services we rely on could become unavailable simultaneously because they share underlying infrastructure.

A few weeks later, Microsoft's global outage brought that very issue into focus.

I returned to The Conversation to examine what was known about the disruption, what might have gone wrong, and why its consequences extended well beyond missed emails and meetings.

As further disruptions followed in the same week, I wrote a third article for The Conversation, examining seven major technology incidents in six days, asking whether they were connected and setting out what should be done about shared dependencies.

The discussion also took place in live interviews with ABC News and ABC Radio and in an interview with news.com.au, while the article was picked up by news organisations internationally. Some republished the original piece; others drew on the analysis in their own reporting, reaching audiences in several languages. Al Jazeera Net then published an Arabic-language feature built largely on the analysis.

This page brings together the original articles, broadcast interviews and selected international coverage that followed.

01 · THE ARTICLES

Three articles for The Conversation: from shared dependencies to a policy response

The October article examined how shared infrastructure can leave apparently separate digital services vulnerable to disruption.

When Microsoft's global outage occurred in November, I revisited that question through a more immediate technical analysis, examining the available evidence, possible causes and consequences for organisations and individuals.

As further failures followed within days, I widened the question in a third article, which compares seven incidents between 12 and 17 November, finds that only one is confirmed as a cyberattack, and proposes three measures: pre-configured and regularly tested local systems for essential services, a say over how global providers roll out changes to them, and independent review of major failures, not only those labelled cyberattacks.

02 · ABC NEWS

Discussing the outage on ABC News

On 13 November 2026, I joined ABC News Channel live to discuss the Microsoft outage, the difficulties involved in identifying its cause, and the consequences for organisations relying on interconnected digital services.

The conversation also explored what businesses can do when essential systems become unavailable, including identifying critical operations, establishing independent alternatives and testing those arrangements before they are needed.

I also discussed the wider implications of digital outages in live ABC Radio interviews, including broadcasts in Melbourne, Brisbane and Toowoomba.

03 · INTERNATIONAL COVERAGE

How international media covered the analysis

As the outage unfolded, journalists in several countries drew on the technical analysis published in The Conversation.

Some used the explanation of Microsoft's automated server health checks, while others focused on the distinction between technical failures and cyberattacks, or the implications of relying on shared digital infrastructure.

The original article was also republished in full by The Independent in the United Kingdom. The following are selected examples of independently written reports that referenced the analysis.

Al Jazeera Net · Arab world · Arabic · 22 November 2026

7 outages in 6 days: why are America’s services collapsing? Experts answer

7 انقطاعات في 6 أيام.. لماذا تتهاوى خدمات أمريكا؟ خبراء يجيبون

Al Jazeera Net built this feature largely on my analysis of the run of outages, rather than citing it in passing. It opens with my point that resilience to cyberattack and resilience to outage are very different things, and goes on to relay my explanations of shared technology layers, routine updates that reach thousands of servers within minutes, and why coincidence, shared weaknesses and deliberate attack are not mutually exclusive. It closes with my advice to users and institutions on keeping essentials available offline and relying on official provider channels.
