In print and online

22 November 2026
Al Jazeera Net · Arabic-language feature

7 outages in 6 days: why are America’s services collapsing? Experts answer (opens in a new tab)7 انقطاعات في 6 أيام.. لماذا تتهاوى خدمات أمريكا؟ خبراء يجيبون

Al Jazeera Net, in an Arabic-language feature headlined “7 outages in 6 days: why are America’s services collapsing? Experts answer”, builds its explanation of the recent run of technology failures largely on my analysis, with comments from other experts alongside. It opens with my point that resilience to cyberattack and resilience to outage are very different things, and relays my explanation that modern services rest on shared layers of technology, that a routine update to one layer can reach thousands of servers within minutes, and that long-hidden faults and automated recovery can make matters worse. It sets out my position that coincidence, shared weaknesses and deliberate attack are not mutually exclusive explanations, that the timing is notable but is not evidence of coordination, and that no public evidence yet links the incidents. It closes with my advice for users and institutions: avoid relying on a single digital service for essential matters, keep important documents and emergency contacts offline, identify the essential operations that can continue without internet or cloud access, and rely on official provider channels, because major outages attract scammers. The article introduces me as an academic in cybersecurity at CQUniversity in Australia.

16 November 2026
news.com.au · Expert interview

Microsoft outage: Big Tech called out after incident cost the world $10 billion (opens in a new tab)

In a report for news.com.au, also published in the Herald Sun, journalist Harrison Christian interviewed several experts on the economic and policy consequences of the Microsoft outage, and included my comments. I advised that organisations should identify their essential operations and establish independent alternatives that have been tested. I also argued that keeping data in Australia is not the same as keeping Australian services running, because locally hosted systems may still depend on global authentication, networking or management infrastructure, so digital sovereignty must include operational independence. Finally, I noted that Microsoft attributed the outage to a network upgrade rather than a cyberattack, and that the biggest risk to essential services may be the changes made to our own systems.
